## Before You Begin

Confirm you have the following locked in:

- The completed **story configuration** (`story-config.md`) from `/novel-genre-picker` — sub-genre and stacked plot archetypes
- The completed **story seed** (`story-seed.md`) from `/novel-story-seed-writer` — C/W/D/A/C breakdown, 1-sentence seed, expanded seed

Read both files before generating the outline. The archetypes from `story-config.md` must shape the beat structure, and the story seed must be the narrative source of truth.

If either file is missing, stop and complete those steps first.

---

Generate a novel outline using the Sub-Genre, Plot Archetype stack, and Story Seed from the files above, structured as a 3-Act outline.

So you understand, here is the 3-Act Structure I’d like you to use:

Act 1 – The Setup

- Beat 1: Exposition
- Beat 2: Inciting Incident
- Beat 3: Plot Point 1

---

Act 2 – Confrontation

- Beat 4: Rising Action
- Beat 5: Midpoint
- Beat 6: Plot Point 2

---

Act 3 – Resolution

- Beat 7: Pre-Climax
- Beat 8: Climax
- Beat 9: Denouement

## The 3-Act Structure Examples

### Example #1: The 3-Act structure Example of The Kite Runner

```
**Act 1 – The Setup:**

Beat 1: Exposition

- Amir grows up in Kabul, Afghanistan during the 1970s alongside his loyal and selfless friend, Hassan.
- Despite the friendship, the two boys come from different social classes.
- Amir belongs to the privileged Pashtun community (he’s rich) and Hassan is a member of the Hazara minority (he’s not rich).

Beat 2: Inciting Incident

- Amir's desire for his father's love and approval drives him to enter a local kite-fighting tournament.
- Winning the competition would gain him his father's admiration, but it also puts Hassan in harm's way as his loyal kite runner.

Beat 3: Plot Point 1

- During the kite-fighting tournament, Amir witnesses Hassan being assaulted by a group of bullies led by Assef.
- Amir, paralyzed by fear and conflicting emotions, doesn’t try to help his friend. This haunts him for years.

**Act 2 – Confrontation:**

Beat 4: Rising Action

- Amir's guilt over betraying Hassan strains their relationship.
- The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan further disrupts their lives, forcing Amir and his father to flee to the US as refugees.
- There, Amir begins to rebuild his life, but the guilt and memories of his past torment him.

Beat 5: Midpoint

- Years later, Amir receives a call from an old family friend, Rahim Khan.
- Rahim reveals secrets about his father's past and asks Amir to undertake a dangerous mission, offering him a chance for redemption.

Beat 6: Plot Point 2

- Amir travels back to Afghanistan, where he learns that Hassan and his wife were murdered by the Taliban.
- Their son, Sohrab, is an orphan. Amir discovers that Sohrab is now under Assef's control, and he must confront his old nemesis to rescue Hassan's son.

**Act 3 – Resolution:**

Beat 7: Pre-Climax

- Amir confronts Assef to save Sohrab, who has suffered immense trauma and abuse.
- During the fight, Amir is severely beaten but manages to defeat Assef and rescue the boy.

Beat 8: Climax

- Amir faces the truth about his past actions and their impact on the lives of those he cared for.
- He decides to take Sohrab back to the US, hoping to provide him with a better life and make amends for the mistakes of the past.

Beat 9: Denouement

- Back in the US, Amir adopts Sohrab and tries to create a loving environment for him.
- The scars of their past remain, but Amir begins to heal and find redemption.
- Sohrab, too, starts to recover from his traumatic experiences.
- Their bond grows stronger, reminiscent of the friendship Amir once shared with Hassan.
```

### Example #2: The 3-Act structure Example Harry Potter & Sorcerer’s Stone
```
**Act 1 – The Setup:**

Beat 1: Exposition

- Harry Potter grows up with the nasty Dursley family (his relatives) outside of London after the mysterious death of his parents.
- Magic is real, but Harry doesn’t know it yet. He’s the most famous wizard in the world but doesn’t have a clue.

Beat 2: Inciting Incident

- On Harry's 11th birthday, he receives a letter from Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry inviting him to attend as a student.
- A half-giant, Hagrid, reveals Harry's magical heritage and the story of his parents' death at the hands of the dark wizard Voldemort.

Beat 3: Plot Point 1

- But Harry doesn’t believe he’s a wizard, and Hagrid has to prove it to him.
- Despite the Dursleys' efforts to prevent him from accepting his invitation to Hogwarts, Harry decides to leave with Hagrid for the school.

**Act 2 – Confrontation:**

Beat 4: Rising Action

- At Hogwarts, he makes friends with Ron and Hermione and starts his magical education, adjusting to this strange, wonderful, new world.
- But Harry also faces challenges, from his first Quidditch match to the animosity of a teacher, Snape.
- He discovers that the Philosopher's Stone, a magical object that grants immortality, is hidden within the school.

Beat 5: Midpoint

- Harry, Ron, and Hermione come to believe Snape is after the Stone and start to investigate.
- Snape seemingly tries to kill Harry during a Quidditch match, which they take as confirmation he’s after the Stone.

Beat 6: Plot Point 2

- In the Forbidden Forest, Harry finds Voldemort drinking the blood of a unicorn.
- Voldemort tries to kill Harry but fails.
- Because of this, Harry knows Voldemort is the one who really wants the Stone and that Snape is just helping him.

**Act 3 – Resolution:**

Beat 7: Pre-Climax

- Harry, Ron, and Hermione try to tell Dumbledore what’s going on but he’s away from the school.
- Instead, they decide to beat Snape to the Stone.
- To do so, they fight their way through a series of ever more dangerous trials.

Beat 8: Climax

- In the depths of the Hogwarts dungeons, Harry doesn’t find Snape but Professor Quirrell, who is possessed by the weakened Voldemort.
- Harry defeats Quirrel but Voldemort escapes.

Beat 9: Denouement

- After recovering, Harry learns his House won the school’s House Cup for the first time in years because of he and his friends.
- The Stone is destroyed to protect it from ever falling into Voldemort’s hands.
- Harry heads back to the Dursleys for the summer.
```

### Example #3: The 3-Act structure Example Red Rising
```
**Act 1 – The Setup:**

Beat 1: Exposition

- Darrow, a 16-year-old miner living beneath the surface of Mars, toils with his fellow Reds, the lowest caste in a color-coded society, under the belief they’re making the planet habitable for future generations of humans.
- He’s content with his life but unaware of the deception and oppression his people face by the ruling class, the Golds.

Beat 2: Inciting Incident

- Darrow is robbed of the laurel, a prize he deserved for his prowess in the mines.
- Thus, Darrow's wife, Eo, rebels against the oppressive society and is executed by the Golds.
- Darrow tries to hang himself too but his uncle secretly saves him.

Beat 3: Plot Point 1

- Darrow gets recruited by the rebel group The Sons of Ares.
- They offer him a chance to break free from his past life and join the ranks of the Golds by going through a brutal physical transformation.
- Darrow accepts the offer and begins his training to infiltrate the Gold society.

**Act 2 – Confrontation:**

Beat 4: Rising Action

- Now trained as a Gold, Darrow enters the Institute, a competition held between Gold teenagers to become the most powerful, influential members of society.
- Darrow is forced to kill Julian, a kind boy and the brother of one of Darrow’s few Gold friends.

Beat 5: Midpoint

- Darrow rises fast in his House but eventually is cast out after one too many failures.
- A girl named Mustang saves him.
- Through her, he realizes what he was doing wrong and instead embraces a new way of leadership that the Golds haven’t seen.

Beat 6: Plot Point 2

- But even though he’s doing well in the competition, Darrow learns the Golds running the Institute favor a different boy to win and are willing to cheat to help him do so.
- Darrow now has opponents both within and without the Institute.
- He captures the boy, but he escaped by cutting off his own hand. Darrow didn’t think he’d go that far.

**Act 3 – Resolution:**

Beat 7: Pre-Climax

- A Gold who runs the Institute tries to kill Darrow, but Darrow kills him instead.
- This becomes proof that the Institute is rigged.
- Darrow and his team defeat the Golds who run the Institute.

Beat 8: Climax

- But Darrow sends Mustang to capture the boy they wanted to win and gives her half his army to do so.
- She is the boy’s twin sister.
- But Mustand is true and does the job; they win the Institute.

Beat 9: Denouement

- Mustang and the boy’s father offers to make Darrow his apprentice.
- But this is the man who killed Eo and Darrow hasn’t heard from The Sons of Ares in months.
- The story ends with Darrow agreeing to be his apprentice, believing it’s the most effective way for him to continue infiltrating Gold society.
```
